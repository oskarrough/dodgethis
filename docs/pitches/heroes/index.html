Eight hero pitches

Each kit takes one verb Fletcher (poke and kite) and Mitts (catch and return) don't own. All keep the house rules: anything that touches an enemy shows its hitbox for a cast point of at least 0.3 s, and every dodgeable shot leaves at least 0.45 s to dodge. The test: a first-timer wins something by pressing Q at someone, and a good player gets something the first-timer can't even see.

Names are placeholders, named by mechanic until the setting settles. Reply by code: "H3.W too strong", "cut H6.R", "H1 ✓".

At a glance

Floor and ceiling dots are my read of the pitch, not numbers from it. Floor: how hard the first win is. Ceiling: how much room there is to get better.

For scale: the built two

Every diagram uses one scale, 8 px per metre (the panel is 32 × 17 m from above). Grey dashed shapes in the hero diagrams are these built abilities, live numbers from tune.js.

Build next: Hook, then Parry

1st

H1 Hook

Nothing built or planned can start a fight, and a hook is the purest dodge test there is: one straight shot with a long, readable tell. It reuses the straight shot and forces one new mechanism, pulling another hero, which Shove, Splash and Fuse all need later.

2nd

H3 Parry

The purest timing test and almost free: it's the catch resolver with a 0.1–0.4 s window that throws back at once. With Fletcher and Mitts it gives the throw-and-answer loop its third voice. Build it before anything else that intercepts: two interceptors is the ceiling, or nobody throws.
