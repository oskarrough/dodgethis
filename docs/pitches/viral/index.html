How it spreads

Oskar, 2026-10-10: “it needs a domain, it needs pvp nice online, but online will only go viral once we have some kind of ranked list.” And on the roadmap: a league, “make a team, climb, see who gets rank 1.”

Five parts: the loop that spreads it, the smallest ranked ladder, what blocks a stranger match today, a plan in five playable milestones, and what the eventual name has to do. Each part names one top pick.

Facts link to sources at the bottom. My estimates say so. Reply by code: “R4 ✓”, “cut S6”, “M2 before M1”.

The first ranked list shouldn't wait for nice PvP. Today the host's browser is the server: it decides every result, gets a ~230 ms head start on every dodge, and ends the match when its tab sleeps. A ladder built on that is unfair before it's ever cheated.

So: make every link drop a stranger into a bot's seat (M1), ship a daily gauntlet against bots as the first ranked list (M2, no netcode involved), then fix the plumbing and the host's head start, and only then open PvP ranked and clubs.

1The loop that spreads it

Every game in the research spread the same way: no install, one link or code, and something worth showing someone else. agar.io went from a 4chan post to about 5M daily players on YouTubers alone1; Among Us sat at 30–50 concurrent players for two years until streamers shouted room codes across calls5; Wordle's grid took it from 90 to 2M daily players in three months10.

S1

The link is the lobby

top picksmall
Today
Rooms join by a five-letter code typed into a box; there's no join by link.
Do
dodgethis.0sk.ar/ABCDE skips the splash and opens straight into the host's walking lobby. “Share code” becomes “Copy link”. A dead code lands in solo practice with a notice, never on an error screen. skribbl.io's private rooms work only by link12.
Size
About 2 slices: a Worker route that serves index.html, and a boot path.
S2

Ten seconds, counted

small
Today
Today's dist is 3.2 MB gzipped (scripts and the Rapier WASM), about 1.3 s on a 20 Mbit line. Nobody has timed the rest (WASM init, textures, shader compile). Phones are unproven, and shared links mostly get opened on phones.
Do
Time tap-to-moving on a cold cache and put it in the verify skill. No name prompt before play: a generated sticker (“Brisk Otter”) you can rename later. The first input moves you. A phone at least gets to watch the match it was invited to, with “send this to my computer”.
Size
One slice to measure; the fixes depend on what the measurement shows.
S3

Strangers take a bot's seat

top pickmedium
Today
The lobby already fills empty seats with host-only bots, but lanes refuse new peers. A friend who arrives mid-match waits for a round trip.
Do
Make it the joining rule: anyone who opens a link takes over a bot, mid-match included, inheriting its hero, spot and HP. A solo player is always in a match; a friend's arrival never costs a restart. Rocket League added bots back to fill short Casual games instead of cancelling them8; Fortnite gives new players bots and keeps competitive queues bot-free9.
Bonus
The same handoff run backwards is rejoin (P6): a dropped human's bot holds the seat until they're back.
Size
About 4 slices.
S4

The share-back card

small
Today
index.html has a <title> and nothing else: no og:image, so a pasted link unfurls as bare text.
Do
Preview tags with a key-art image. On the result screen, one tap copies a short card: an emoji strip of the match (win, KOs, dunks, your hero) plus a rematch link to the same room. Wordle's grid spread because it showed the shape of a game without spoiling it, and players were already sharing it by hand before it was built in10.
Size
About 2 slices.
S5

Eight-second clips

largelater
Why
Fall Guys and Among Us spread through streams of chaos that reads at a glance65b. A clip of your last dunk is the strongest share there is. Highlight clips are shelved; replays already reproduce a match, so the hard half exists.
When
After a season is running; a clip with a rank badge beats a clip without one.
S6

A Discord Activity

mediumlater
Why
Discord runs web games inside a voice call as Activities17. That's Among Us's “shout the code” loop with the code removed.
Catch
Activities reach the network through Discord's proxy, so PeerJS and public TURN are likely out. It wants the relay rooms of P5 first.

2Ranked: the smallest ladder that works

Identity without accounts, a rating that handles teams, short seasons, clubs, a ladder that works with three people online, and abuse rules sized for tens of players, not millions.

R1

A name and a key, no account

small
Do
First play mints a random secret in localStorage and a public player id. The server stores the id, the name and a hash of the secret. You only pick a name the first time you'd appear on a list. To carry a rank to another device: a one-time transfer link or QR now, a passkey once someone asks. No email, no password.
Why
Every .io game in the research asked for nothing but a name4. Lichess lets you play anonymously15. Accounts are friction at the exact moment a stranger is deciding whether to stay.
Today
Humans have no names at all, just P1/P2 tags.
R2

OpenSkill underneath, points on top

top pick (PvP)small
Do
OpenSkill (Weng-Lin, MIT, an npm package) rates teams natively16; Glicko-2 is built for one-on-one and wants 10–15 games per rating period13. Rocket League runs on the same Weng-Lin mu and sigma7b. Sort the board by μ − 3σ, the conservative estimate: it starts low and rises as the system learns you, so a newcomer sees themselves climb. Show tiers, not raw numbers; Rocket League hides MMR below Grand Champion7.
Cut
Separate trophy-style points (Brawl Stars' Trophy Road11) only if tiers alone feel dead.
R3

Four-week seasons, and rank 1 lives in the lobby

small
Do
Four weeks while balance changes weekly; Krunker runs monthly ranked seasons too3. Soft reset: σ widens and tiers restart, the way Rocket League places returning players again7. Last season's tier becomes a sticker on your name tag.
The fun bit
The menu is the game, so the ladder is a scoreboard you walk past in the lobby, and last season's rank 1 stands on a plinth beside the hero stands. That answers “see who gets rank 1” without a menu screen.
R4

The daily gauntlet: a ranked list that needs no one online

top pickmedium
Do
Every day, one seed, one map, one set of Hard bots for everyone. Score: time to the enemy core, with KOs as the tie-break. The board resets at midnight UTC, and the card reads “Gauntlet #12 · 4:31 · rank 37 of 210”.
Why first
It's a real “who's rank 1” with three players in the world, it needs no netcode, and it's checkable: replays already reproduce a match from its inputs (the replay viewer says so when a build doesn't), so the top scores can be re-run in bun overnight on the office box. GeoGuessr's ranked is a weekly ladder of small divisions14; Wordle proved a daily puzzle with a shareable score is enough10.
Catch
Re-running needs the same build and WASM as the player had, so submissions carry the build id and get checked against that revision. Rapier's WASM inside a Worker is unproven, so verify on the box, not at the edge.
R5

Clubs: the league, summed

medium
Do
A club is a name, a three-letter tag and up to 30 members. A club's score is the sum of its members' best season results, gauntlet included. The tag sits on your name sticker. Brawl Stars clubs hold 3011b; Krunker's Turf Wars counts only a clan's top 203b, and that cap stops one huge clan from just winning on headcount.
Why summed
It doesn't need three clubmates online at once. A ladder of fixed trios (“make a team” in the strict sense) comes when queues hold enough humans for 3v3.
R6

Matchmaking with few players online

medium
Do
Queue for 20 s at a widening rating window, then fill every empty seat with bots, labelled as bots. Rating only moves on human-against-human outcomes: one human a side plus four bots is still a fair one-on-one read. Gather people with a nightly “ranked hour” and a countdown on the splash.
Not
Disguised bots. Fall Guys and Brawl Stars never confirmed theirs6b, and players spent years suspecting them. Fortnite admits its bots and keeps them out of competitive play9; that's the honest version.
R7

Abuse rules at our scale

small
Hole
The host's browser runs the sim, so it could report any result it likes.
Do
Every human client reports the result, and a mismatch voids the match. Rating gained from the same opponent is capped per day, which stops farming an alt. New players are provisional (σ already handles it). Keep ranked inputs for spot checks, and put Cloudflare Turnstile on claiming a name.
Later
Running the sim inside a Durable Object is the real fix. Do it when someone actually cheats, not before. With tens of players, a cheater would be a nice problem to have. Krunker needed a native client for anti-cheat only at real scale3.
R8

On Cloudflare: one Ladder Durable Object

top picksmall
Shape
/api/ladder/* goes to one Ladder Durable Object with SQLite storage, the same pattern as LobbyDirectory in worker/index.js. It holds players, matches, ratings, seasons, clubs and gauntlet scores. A single writer makes rating updates atomic with no locking. Later, a Matchmaker object holds the queue on hibernating WebSockets. Replays go to R2 if they're kept at all.
Why not D1
D1's row prices are identical19. It earns its place only when a second service needs the data, or one object outgrows its storage. Neither is close.
Cost
Free plan: 100k Durable Object requests and 100k rows written a day18, which covers the ladder alone for a long time. My estimate for 1,000 players playing 20k matches a month: about 260k row writes and 40k requests, inside the $5 Paid plan's allowance (50M writes, 1M requests). Relay rooms (P5) are what push it onto Paid.
R9

TinyJoin, weighed seriously

not for the ladder
What it is
A Rust/WASM relational database that runs in the browser, saved to OPFS, 354 KiB20. Nicely made, and smaller and faster than SQLite in the browser by its own benchmarks.
Fit
A ladder is one shared truth every player trusts. TinyJoin has “no server, and no sync”: it never leaves the player's machine20b. Anything a player can write locally is a rank they can edit. It can't hold identity either: storage the browser may evict is no place for the key to your rank.
Maturity
v0.6.0, two months old (repo created 2026-08-09, 33 stars on GitHub), “experimental and verified on Chromium only”. Releases may require recreating the database rather than migrating it20b.
Cost, lock-in
Free and MIT, no lock-in. A Durable Object is Cloudflare-only, but its SQL is plain SQLite and moving off it is a dump.
Verdict
The ladder lives in the Durable Object. TinyJoin's only plausible job is a local cache of your own match history, and localStorage does that today. Look again if it ships sync.

3What blocks a stranger match today

From network.md and the Shared play section of moba-plan.md. Ordered by how soon a stranger hits it.

# Gap What a stranger sees Fix Size
P1 No join by link A code to type, from a chat they're reading on a phone S1 small
P2 Version lock Every push to main deploys, and mismatched protocols refuse to join. A friend's stale tab can't join your fresh one. Rooms carry a build id; a mismatched joiner gets a “new version, reload” button small
P3 No names “P2 killed you” R1 small
P4 Flagfall is offline only Shared handoffs force Overthrow; the map with Dunk can't be played together Flagfall's replica medium
P5 Plumbing on borrowed goodwill Public PeerJS signalling and OpenRelay TURN, with “no production reliability guarantee”. Strangers behind strict NATs need TURN. Relay rooms, below medium
P6 No rejoin A refresh or Wi-Fi blip loses your seat for good A bot holds the seat for 60 s (S3 backwards) medium
P7 The host is the server Host alt-tabs, the browser throttles its timers to about once a second21, and after 10 s everyone's session ends. Host leaves: same. Sim in a Durable Object (R7 later), or host migration large
P8 The host's head start A guest's own click moves after about 233 ms at 100 ms ping, 333 ms at 200. Only the host dodges a centred Q on sight. In a dodge game, that decides fights. Local-hero prediction, already designed (git show 655bd1d) large
P5

Relay rooms: one Durable Object per room

top pickmedium
Do
Every player opens a WebSocket to the room's Durable Object, which forwards host envelopes and guest intents. The host stays authoritative and link.js barely notices, but PeerJS, STUN, TURN and NAT trouble are gone. The room knows who's seated, so rejoin (P6) and build checks (P2) live there naturally. Once the room is the hub, moving the sim into it (P7) is a later step, not a rewrite.
Cost
Incoming WebSocket messages bill 20 to a request; outgoing are free; duration bills at 128 MB per object18. My estimate for a full 3v3 of humans over 10 minutes: about 250 incoming messages a second (intents plus host envelopes), so 150k messages, 7.5k billed requests and 75 GB-s. At 1,000 such matches a month, about $1 over the $5 plan; at 10k, about $20. One-human-a-side ranked costs a fraction of that.
Catch
The Free plan's 100k requests a day covers only about a dozen full matches, so relay rooms mean the $5 plan. It adds a hop to the nearest Cloudflare edge, usually a few ms. Measure it before believing it.

PvP ranked waits on P8. Asking strangers to climb a ladder where the host dodges and they can't is how a game earns “rigged” in its first Reddit thread.

4Five milestones, each playable

Sizes in build slices of about 30 minutes, the unit from moba-plan. My estimates, before any surprises.

M1 first ~10 slices

Link in

  • Join by link (S1), generated names (R1's first half)
  • Take a bot's seat in the lobby and lane (S3); rejoin holds it (P6)
  • Preview tags and the result card (S4); build-id reload (P2)
Send a link to your phone or the laptop, open it, and be moving in the lane in under 10 s, in place of a bot.
M2 ~10 slices

The daily gauntlet

  • Player key and name (R1), Ladder Durable Object (R8)
  • Daily seed, score submit, overnight replay check of the top 20 (R4)
  • Board on the splash and in the lobby; gauntlet card
Play today's gauntlet, see your name at rank N, send the card to someone and watch them try to beat it.
M3 ~12 slices

Relay rooms

  • Room Durable Object replaces PeerJS and TURN (P5)
  • Rejoin through the room; Flagfall online (P4)
  • Workers Paid plan from here
A match against someone on a phone hotspot across town, with a refresh mid-match that puts them back in their seat.
M4 ~18 slices

Ranked, season 1

  • Local-hero prediction first (P8), about half the slices
  • Queue with bot fill after 20 s (R6), OpenSkill (R2), both sides report (R7)
  • Four-week season, the lobby scoreboard and plinth (R3)
Queue as a guest and dodge a Q on sight; meet a human or bots after 20 s; see your tier move.
M5 ~8 slices

The league

  • Clubs with tags, a summed club board (R5)
  • End of season: the rank 1 player and the top club on the plinth
  • The sim moves into the Durable Object only if P7 or cheating bites
Make a club with two friends and see it on the board.

Clips (S5) and a Discord Activity (S6) come after M4, once there's a rank worth showing off.

5What the name and domain need to do

The domain stays dodgethis.0sk.ar until the rebrand lands; picking the name is Oskar's call. What it has to do:

N1

Survive being said out loud

Among Us spread through voice calls and streams. Someone hears the name once and types it right: no doubled letters to guess, no puns that need spelling out, one obvious way to write it.

N2

Make a short link that looks safe

Room links are the loop, so name.gg/ABCDE must fit in a chat line and look like a game, not phishing. A .gg reads as a game; a .com reads as trustworthy to everyone else. A subdomain of a personal domain reads as neither.

N3

Be findable

Searching the name plus “game” should land on us, not on a band, a common word or another game. Agar.io's name came from a 4chan commenter1; what made it stick was that it was the only thing with that name.

N4

Land before identity does

top pick

localStorage belongs to one origin. If player keys ship on dodgethis.0sk.ar and the game moves later, every rank is stranded behind a transfer step most players will skip. Either pick the name before M2, or build M2's transfer link to double as the move. Room paths stay domain-free, so the old domain redirects /ABCDE straight across.
