# World

What the MOBA is about, under the mechanics. Three premises, then the pick, worked out against [moba-heroes.md](moba-heroes.md), [moba-look.md](moba-look.md), [moba-front.md](moba-front.md) and [moba-lobby.md](moba-lobby.md). Nothing here changes a number. **Settled** means it reads straight off what's built or already in the docs; **Oskar** marks a choice that's his.

## Three premises

**1. Over the fence.** Everything thrown that never came back lands in the same place: every ball over the fence, every arrow lost in long grass, every mitten off its string, every cardboard cutout left in a skip when the shop changed its window. Here, being let go is how you're born. The pale planet low in the sky is the Yard they all fell from, and every few minutes something else comes over: the Ball. Nobody is anybody's toy, and nobody is waiting to be fetched.

**2. The soft accord.** A desert left by a war nobody remembers the reason for. The peace was written by the only negotiators both sides trusted, children, and it has two clauses: nothing hard may be made, and every dispute is settled by a game. So the whole civilisation went soft (card, felt, rubber, sackcloth), and playground rules hold like law: caught is out, out walks the touchline, red light means freeze. A match is a court case and the cores are the disputed property.

**3. The unfinished game.** The first game ever played ended on a throw so hard it never came down: that's the pale planet, still rising. Nobody was called out, so the bell never rang, and the schoolyard has been eroding into desert for ten thousand years of afternoon. The figures are what the players' kit became, the Ball is a chip off the moon, and every match is another attempt to finish.

**The pick is 1.** It's the only one the four existing heroes already fit without a story bent round them: Mitts is a lost glove, Skip is a cardboard cutout, Carom is a racket from a four-walled room, and the Ball literally comes over the fence. It explains the toy materials (they're what play is made of) and gives every hero a place they came from. It keeps 2's best idea, playground rules as law, as local custom, and 3 survives as a myth the Drain tells about the Yard.

## Lost Property

- **The place.** A pale desert under the Yard. The sand is every grain that went home in a shoe and got tipped out on a doormat; the mesas are sandcastles old enough to have forgotten it; the broken arch is a croquet hoop. Settled. Oskar: the name (Lost Property, the Over, Yonder).
- **The Slab** is the lane: a piece of schoolyard tarmac that came over whole, painted lines, hedges and bollards included. It's the only flat ground for days, and the plaza's painted circle is where Balls come down, so every quarrel ends up on it. The sandlot is a second patch that fell, with its own game. The maps are named for their games: Overthrow is played on the Slab, Flagfall on the sandlot. Settled.
- **The Ball** is something that just went over the fence in the Yard; you see its shadow first. A fresh Ball is still warm from being played with, so it hits harder than anything made here, until it goes cold after 45 s. Settled: that's the dropping shadow and the Ball's life, read as story.
- **Keeps** are the cores. A match settles a dispute for keeps, the way marbles are played: each captain sets down a keep, a chandelier drop worth more than either team, and builds sandcastles in front of it. Break theirs and finders keepers does the rest. The stake is whatever they were arguing about: water, the next Ball, the right to name a dune. Minions are strays, small lost things with no names yet (a frisbee, a stick, a cone, a block), which follow the nearest keep and walk at whatever it points at. Settled.
- **Teams are picked on the day.** Two captains pick sides, so friends play against each other and six Fletchers is a legal Saturday. Red and blue are bibs out of the Cupboard's bag, not allegiances. Settled: it's why mirror matches need no excuse.
- **Quarters** are the factions: where you landed is who you are, and teams cut across them. The Long Grass holds things lost outdoors (arrows, kites, shuttlecocks): sun-faded canvas and twine, tall-tale tellers who sleep out. The Cupboard is the PE store (mitts, rackets, beanbags, whistles): rubber, leather and mesh, drilled and loyal, keepers of the rules. The Window is shop display (cutouts, mascots, mannequins): card, gloss and tape, made to be looked at and still performing. The Drain is everything down the grating (marbles, keys, rings): glass, tin and enamel, old money that lends out the keeps. Oskar: names and how many.

## The four

- **Fletcher** (Long Grass): a quiver that went over after its arrows, one summer at a time. Restless, cheerful, never where you left him. "Thirty-seven arrows went over that fence. I've found three." "Seen one? Green fletching. Answers to nothing."
- **Mitts** (Cupboard): the catcher's mitt that missed the one ball that went over, and went after it. She hasn't dropped anything since. "I dropped one once. Once." On a return: "Ta. Have it back."
- **Carom** (Cupboard): a squash racket from a basement court, raised in a room with four walls and no sky. Brilliant by a pillar, nervous in the open road. "Walls are just friends who stand still." "Too much sky. Nothing to argue with."
- **Skip** (Window): a life-size cutout of a captain from a sports-shop window, nine years a picture of someone who plays. Out, not gone, is what a cutout is for. "I pick the last one first." On Back in!: "Nobody sits out on my team."

## What it changes in the art

- **The prop is the person.** Each hero is the lost thing it carries, and the body grew round it from its quarter's material. The prop is the original, worn and a little oversized; the body never outshines it. This is moba-look's one-prop rule with a reason behind it. Settled.
- **Team colour is a bib**, tape or a sash put on for the day, plus the foot disc. A hero's own colours stay out of red, blue and gold. Settled.
- **Sandcastles.** Towers get a bucket's ridged drum and a paper cocktail flag, forts the turret mould's crenels, and a fallen structure slumps into a sand heap with its flag beside it instead of rubble. The core keeps its facets: a chandelier drop on stacked buckets. Oskar, since it rewrites moba-look's silhouettes and rubble.
- **The Slab's edge** is torn tarmac with a run of chain-link on the boundary and the desert below; the paint stays worn and quiet, so the ground rule holds. The Ball is a pebbled playground ball in ammo gold, shadow first. Settled.
- **The Yard** in the front-end sky gets a faint hatched fence and court on its face, legible only at hero select. Oskar.

## The next six

The roster caps at ten. Ranked by build order: what the four lack first, and cheapest to build on what exists.

1. **Yo-yo** (no quarter, hook): the only thing here that always comes back, so nobody trusts it. Hooks a hero and returns to the throw point with them.
2. **Mascot** (Window, tank): an empty foam suit with a giant foam finger; nobody has seen inside, including Mascot. Shoves, and gets cheered louder the more it's hit.
3. **Kite** (Long Grass, mage): snapped its string and kept the tail; flies over hedges and ties a hero to the spot.
4. **Ref** (Cupboard, support): a whistle on a lanyard, picked for a side and visibly unhappy about it. Blows for fouls: silences whoever just cast.
5. **Taw** (Drain, bruiser): the shooter marble, old money. Draws a ring, and whatever he knocks out of it he keeps for a moment: a shield, a pocketed shot, the Ball.
6. **Shuttle** (Long Grass, assassin): a garden shuttlecock that always lands nose-first; dives on an arc, and landing on a hero resets the dive.

Yo-yo and Mascot come next because nobody in the four can start a fight: Mitts holds a line, Carom finishes, Skip saves. A hook is the purest dodge test there is, one straight shot with a long tell, and Mascot is the body a 5v5 needs to stand behind. Both reuse the straight shot, the dash and the swap. Taw touches Pocket, shields and the Ball at once, and Shuttle doubles Carom's job with reset maths on top, so they go last. Oskar: the order.
