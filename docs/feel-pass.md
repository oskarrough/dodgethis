### The cast tool

`bun run cast <hero> [--steps …] [--clip]` (`scripts/cast.js`, with `scripts/cast-page.js` evaluated in the page and `scripts/capture.js` for the lock and boot, shared with `bun run shot`). It stages a row on Overthrow's centre line (`--at x,z` elsewhere), then sends what a player's hardware sends: a real pointer at the target, `KeyboardEvent`s for Q W E R (held, then released), a real right-click for the basic, S to stop. `--steps` takes `basic`, slots or ability ids with `:enemy|ally|self|miss`, `:gap=`, `:hold=`, `:tail=`; `cast.json` also keeps each step's raw fact log. Each step asserts that the press became that action, nothing was denied or cancelled, the cast landed on the cursor, and a `miss` hit nobody; `cast.json` carries the authored cast lock, activity phases (`cast:`, `attack:`, `dash:`, `channel:`, `stance:`, `motion`), first hit and settle time, in sim seconds from the cast.

Speed, said plainly. The sim never runs on the browser's clock: the script steps the real app loop one 1/60 s tick at a time and, with `--clip`, renders and grabs the canvas every `--every` ticks (default 2), so the clip plays at authored speed whatever the load. A plain screen recording on this box (load near 40, software rendering) ran the sim at roughly a fifth of real time, which is why the balloon proof was retimed 3x. The clip has no HUD and is not a crowded fight. Frame rate, input latency and hardware feel are not measured and cannot be from here. Cost on the loaded box (load 30–40): Undine's basic plus Balloon hit and miss with a clip took 50 s, Oolong's whole kit with a clip 194 s for 10 s of video, about half a second per rendered frame; a kit without a clip is about a minute. A page evaluation is killed at 30 s, so steps run in chunks; the page's own animation frames are stopped for the run, because holding the sim with a pause makes the app read each of its own frames as a neutral input and cancel a cast in flight.

`bun run capture -- <command>` runs any browser work under `/tmp/dodgethis-capture.lock` (flock, so a crashed holder frees it); `bun run capture --status` says who holds it. `shot` and `cast` take it themselves, waiting up to `DT_CAPTURE_WAIT` seconds (900).

Proof hooks added: `dt.game.moba.facts` (the last 512 facts, newest last; `tune.proof.facts`) and `dt.game.render()` (draws now, so a proof stepping the sim itself can grab the canvas).

### Shared primitives (all opt-in; existing callers unchanged)

- `effects.spokes(at, { color, ink, count, from, to, inner, width, life, y, opacity, turn, delay })`: radial tapered strokes thrown out and thinning, flat on the ground, each set drawn over a slightly wider ink set so it reads on pale stone (`ink: null` for none). For landings, calls and releases (Vault, Dive, Rain, Lob, Pinball, Rally ring, Mend). Geometry cached by shape, disposed with the kit. As an `impact` recipe field (`spokes: { … }`) it scales with the impact feel like the flare does and is turned by the tick, so replays draw the same burst.
- `effects.streak(from, to, { color, ink, width, life, y, toY, opacity })`: a straight ink-edged stroke that thins and fades where it was drawn. The line a dash or a throw leaves (Flying kick, Dive, Slide, Vault, Throw). Generalises the vault streak in `skills-view.js`, which keeps its own.
- `src/modes/moba/pose-curve.js`: `poseWindup(progress)` and `poseStrike(fire, settle)` for hero `animate()` hooks. Six hero views lean linearly with `progress` today; these give a quick coil that holds taut and a snap that overshoots and settles. Pure functions; no state to dispose.

Checked: effects age by presentation `dt` and clear on `reset()`/`dispose()` (restart, rematch); nothing here reads the sim, so guests draw from the same facts; marks that need interpolation belong in an ability view's `extras` using `tick + alpha` (see `balloon-view.js`), as the balloon does.

### What the peer games teach, and how far to trust it

Sources I could open are marked. I have not played these games and do not quote timings.

- Super Smash Bros. (Sakurai, official). A strike freezes both fighters briefly and scales with damage; the struck one shakes more, the striker a little; the hurtbox stays put while the drawing shakes; shake is scaled by camera distance ([Famitsu column vol. 490, fan translation](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/)). His hit-stop video lists eight tricks, among them keep the hit box fixed, shake horizontally on the ground, vary the shake with camera distance, lessen it gradually, interpolate into the damage pose and keep the attacker moving a little ([Nintendo Wire summary](https://nintendowire.com/news/2022/12/12/this-week-in-sakurai-12-5-12-11-fine-tuning-hit-stop-and-cheating-the-system/)). Applies here as the existing per-unit `hold`, `skid` and distance-scaled `shake`/`nudge` in `createImpacts`: a local, view-only hold on the two bodies, never a global stop and never the sim.
- Heroes of the Storm (Blizzard). I found no talk or article on telegraphs; the evidence is thin. A 2018 developer AMA is reported as saying visual clarity and team-fight readability are "always super important" and that balancing FX and animation between "looking amazing and reading well" is tricky (a search excerpt of [Icy Veins' recap](https://www.icy-veins.com/forums/topic/40547-heroes-of-the-storm-post-blizzcon-2018-developer-ama-recap/); the page itself refused me). The 2019 GDC talk on adding Orphea is described as about powerful silhouettes and intuitive motion, "gameplay first" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-how-blizzard-brought-orphea-to-i-heroes-of-the-storm-i-), [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025744/Bringing-Orphea-to-Heroes-of); I read only the description, not the talk). Applies: the kit's loudest mark is the telegraph, residue drops to quiet and ends with the hitbox, and a pose must change the hero's silhouette from the match camera.
- Hades (Supergiant). No primary talk found. Official material is a link to a Twitter breakdown of the effects by Josh Barnett that I could not view ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/a-behind-the-scenes-look-at-the-effects-in-hades/)), and patch notes that list readability fixes to effects ([Supergiant blog](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/5/)). Everything below is therefore my reading of the shipped game, not a sourced claim: combat effects are flat graphic strokes (arcs, rays, rings, a few hard shapes) rather than particle clouds, which is what `spokes`, `streak`, `smear` and `ring` are for, and it fits look.md's "flat shapes with an ink keyline, no lingering particle clouds".

Direction for builders, from the above:

1. Anticipation is a shape, not a wait. Spend a cast point on a coil that changes the silhouette (`poseWindup`), release with a snap (`poseStrike`), then recover. Do not lengthen anything just to display it.
2. The hit is local. Bodies take a short view-only hold, a skid and a distance-scaled shake; the hit area and the sim never move.
3. One dominant graphic mark per action, drawn as flat strokes with ink. Anything that lingers is quieter than the telegraph and ends with the hitbox.
4. Give each hero a distinct rhythm and silhouette: precise and quick, heavy and delayed, elastic, areal. A kit that moves one way is one note.
5. Judge it as a fight: crowded, at the match camera, in grayscale. The cast clip shows shapes and sequence for one hero; it does not prove a fight reads.
