# Dungeon

A WoW-style dungeon in the moba mode: Altamesa, one open plateau a party roams freely, with hand-placed packs and seven encounters. The Warden (`mechanics/dungeon/dungeon-warden.js`, numbers in `tune.warden`) is the boss body that most encounters build on; its swing and slam live in `abilities/{swing,slam}/`.

## Altamesa

Altamesa is a proof of concept for a longer dungeon: one open plateau of sand and red stone (`?mode=moba&map=altamesa&play`, in `maps/altamesa/`) where a party of up to five walks in from the west, fights through hand-placed packs of guards and fells a series of Wardens.

The packs (`mechanics/packs/`) stand where the layout puts them, each made of melee, ranged and brute guards on team `N`. When any one of them notices a hero the whole pack wakes together, and if the party flees the guards follow only so far before they turn home, heal to full once they are calm, and wait. A few walk a patrol loop between two points until they spot someone. A pack that dies stays dead.

How hard the mesa fights back is one number, the dial (`mechanics/dungeon/dungeon-dial.js`, `tune.dial`). Every hero who walks in counts one, bots as much as friends, and the difficulty plate you stand on in the party lobby adds to it: Easy comes a little short of the party, Normal fights you as if one more hero had come with you, and Hard as if one and a half had. The Wardens, the pack guards and Barquero's bardinos read their health from it, and each Warden's own health is set for a party of three on Normal, its blows for Easy. Health follows the party steeply: a pair meets three fifths of a Warden and a lone hero a fifth, because the bots cannot get one hero past Fornax or Espejo with more. The weight of the blows does not move with the party, since a party loses more heroes to the number of bodies than to the weight of a blow, but it does move with the difficulty: on Normal the mesa strikes twice as hard as on Easy and on Hard two and a half times, so a party of three on Normal falls somewhere in the second wing if nobody heals, and on Hard at Fornax. Your bots keep their wits whatever you pick: the dial weighs the mesa, never your friends. In bot runs Easy takes about five minutes, Normal about eight and Hard about ten; a lone hero clears Easy and Normal and may fall on Hard. How fast a fight's clock runs and how many of its mechanics turn at once are kept on the dial too, at nothing for now.

To tune the dial, remember that bots replay a lost fight identically: a Warden too strong for a party becomes a wall, the same wipe again and again, rather than a slow win, and the walls sit sharply (a lone hero stops at Fornax, Carillon or Espejo once the dial leaves them more than a fifth of their health). Sweep `bun run simulate --map altamesa --summary --party N --difficulty <spot> --heroes ibis,kappa` and `--heroes mandador,lasso` on seeds 2 and 3, and set a Warden's health from the shell with `--set encounters.<id>.hp=N`. To ask whether a party needs its healer, field it with `--lineup kappa,oolong,salvia` and play a Salvia who never heals with `--set mend.bot.deficit=1e9 --set ward.bot.saveHp=0`; an `--idle` seat will not do, since the bots keep a hand at Manos for it and wait at the gate forever. A wipe is not a loss, as the party simply tries again, so count the runs that end walled at one Warden.

Each fight is an encounter (`encounters/<id>/`, see `extending.md`), and the layout places seven along the road: Manos, Tinaja and Botijo, Fornax, Barquero, Carillon, Espejo and Gnomon. Fornax and Barquero hold the shade wing to the north, Carillon and Espejo the sun wing to the south, and Gnomon waits at the top of the stair in the east. The dungeon run (`mechanics/dungeon/dungeon-run.js`) lets several of these pulls stand open at once. A Warden that falls stays fallen, and the heroes who died in that fight rise a few seconds later at their waystones. A wipe puts back only the Warden you were fighting. Gnomon sleeps until all six other Wardens are dead, with the two wings in whichever order the party likes and any pack free to be skipped, and its death ends the run with a win for the party.

Manos, the hands at the gate, is the first encounter with a fight of its own (`encounters/manos/`). A wall runs across the mesa from rim to rim, and its one door stands shut until three great hands on the sand before it are lit at the same time. A hand fills while a hero stands on it and stays lit for ten seconds after they leave, so three heroes may hold one each while a lone hero must run the circuit. The first light wakes the Tiestos by the walls, who lob shards at the nearest hero; a red ring shows where each will land, and whoever still stands there is hurt. Bots share the hands out among themselves and step aside from the rings.

Tinaja and Botijo (`encounters/tinajaBotijo/`) hold Tagoror, the central court, two jars who share one potter's wheel. Pull one and both come. While they stand within a few metres of each other an ink line binds them and each takes only half of every blow, so drag them apart. When one breaks, the other goes to the wheel: it stands still and strikes no one, a red ring fills round its fallen kin, and after ten seconds the kin rises whole. Kill the second before the wheel turns. A wipe mends them both. Between them they hit at less than one Warden's weight.

Fornax (`encounters/fornax/`) is the shade wing's first fight, a long hot hall where the only cool ground is the shade of an awning that walks from one end to the other and back. While Fornax is pulled, anyone in the hall outside the shade gathers Heat, a stack a second, and every stack burns; five stacks bake a hero solid for three seconds. In the shade the stacks cool away. A blue pool on the floor shows the shade, and a red ring round each hero's feet closes as their Heat builds. Fight it from the shade, and bring the fight with you as the shade moves. Bots step back into the shade at two stacks and stay until they have cooled.

Barquero (`encounters/barquero/`) ends the shade wing, and you do not fight him: the old boatman waits at his ferry for Tara, the clay idol, and you must bring it to him down a dotted road. Tara rolls only while someone stands in its ring, turning the ring blue, and coasts on for three seconds after the last one steps out. From the first push, bardinos pour over the dunes two at a time and run at whoever pushes. So one pushes while the rest keep the bardinos off, and a lone hero pushes, turns to fight, and pushes again. If the whole party falls, the bardinos scatter and Tara rolls home. Bots give the push to the first of them by name and send the rest after the bardino nearest the idol.

Carillon (`encounters/carillon/`) opens the sun wing: a Warden asleep inside a great bronze bell, which lets through only a fifth of every blow. Every ten seconds of the fight it tolls. A red ring is warned round it for two seconds, then lands, and whoever stands inside is struck hard and stunned for a second. Each toll cracks the bronze, and for six seconds after, its rim turns blue and Carillon takes half again of every blow. So stand outside the ring, then rush in and strike. Bots step out of every warned toll, the one Carillon is hitting too. (The deck has the Ball cracking the bell; that waits until the Ball can be placed by an encounter.)

Espejo (`encounters/espejo/`) closes the sun wing: a Warden with a mirror for a face, which turns slowly toward the hero it hates most. A blow struck into the mirror lands at half, and a quarter of it is thrown back at whoever struck. So one hero holds its gaze and the rest strike at its back, where the blue arc does not reach. Every twelve seconds it stops for three to polish its face, ringed in ink, and takes every blow whole; then it forgets every grudge and turns on the nearest hero. Bots that are not its target walk round to its back. Mid-pull a hurt bot fights on rather than run for a waystone, since a waystone mends no one while a fight stands open.

Gnomon (`encounters/gnomon/`) waits at the top of the stair, the pin of the great sundial, and it trades in time and shadow. Every six seconds its shadow falls like an hour hand, aimed at each hero in turn: it lengthens from Gnomon's foot for a second and a half, then lands, striking and holding everyone it covers. Step aside out of the hand and it passes you by. Round its foot a dial of twelve marks fills toward the hour it turns back. When the dial fills, every twenty seconds and blue for the last three, Gnomon takes back the life it had ten seconds before. So strike hard between the turnings, or the dial undoes your work.

A fight should last about a minute and carry real risk, so a party without a healer such as Salvia must dodge well. The smoke test sends a party of three bots without a healer through the whole run by each wing, on Easy.

Each wing boss leaves a boon when it falls, and the party carries it to Gnomon, where each one changes the fight. Lupa from Carillon makes Gnomon take a quarter more from every blow. Azogue from Espejo halves the length of its shadow, so a hero who stands back is out of reach. Bizcocho from Fornax softens its blows and its shadow. Tara from Barquero makes it turn back only half as far. A run carries all four, since Gnomon wakes only once both wings have fallen. The goal card in the top bar lists the boons you carry, and the numbers are in `encounters/gnomon/gnomon-tune.js`.

Eight waystones stand along the road. Walk up to one and it attunes, so that anyone who falls rises at the last stone you passed instead of at the gate. Stand beside it while no boss fight is on and it mends you, from nothing to full in five seconds. The hourglass in the top bar counts the run against three marks, Glass, Clay and Stone, and beneath the top bar a row of seven small marks, one per Warden, is the goal: each is struck through when its Warden falls, and Gnomon's wears a lock until the stair opens. Bots fight any woken pack near them and otherwise walk to the next Warden. They fight with their whole kit, as in the lanes (Rain on a pack, Mend on a friend, Stagger and Swig when the blows come thick), and give it up only while they stand in a warned ring, where stepping out comes first. A Warden that has not been pulled is a place to walk to, not a danger to weigh.

## Where it is going

Altamesa is played by one to five heroes, friends and optional bots together, and the Wardens must be a fair fight for whoever turns up; a bot counts as a player.

Two questions sit under everything else. Does the dungeon meet you, so the fight fits the party, its skill and its mood? Does it remember you, so the next visit is not the same as the last? The first is the dial above: party size sets it, the players may turn it up or down, and a difficulty setting is only a named spot on it. Health and blows already ride on it; what tempo and the number of mechanics running at once should do is the open part.

What remembers you should reward curiosity, never attendance: no streaks, timers or chests, and nothing a child feels they must log in for. New choices are better than bigger numbers.

Underneath boons and talents lies one question: what choices of playstyle do players get, when, and for how long? A choice can come before a run, at a milestone inside it (one of three, as Heroes of the Storm offers talents every few levels) or between runs, and it can last a fight, a run or for good. No dead levels: packs give XP and every level brings a talent choice from a tree that is visible from the start, as in World of Warcraft, so you can see what is coming and look forward to it, while Wardens give the big moments. Some packs stand off the road as an optional farm, as in Warcraft III's custom maps: clearing them makes the Wardens easier, skipping them is a dare, and since packs stay dead the farm has an end. Bots follow the player's lead and never farm on their own. The leading idea: build choices are made inside a run and reset with it, while what lasts is the menu itself, which grows as Wardens fall. Other ways to remember you are still open: mastery itself as the only progress, and the mesa changing where a Warden fell.

Talents are built for Ibis and Oolong; the rest of the cast follows. A row opens at each level from 2 to 7 and you take one of its three; T (the pad's d-pad up) opens the whole tree beside play, with the rows still ahead shown in full, and the chip under the unit frame wakes when a row waits on you. The pick is an intent like a cast, so it is taken while stunned or dead and never stops the fight, and bots take their hero's preferred column at once. Picks last the run. Each hero's tree is data in its folder (`heroes/<id>/<id>-talents.js`, numbers in its tune), and `talents.js` applies a pick by giving that one hero a restated copy of its kit; trees exist only in a dungeon run, so the lanes stay as they were.

The numbers today: a Warden's fall pays 400 XP (`warden.xp`), a guard 100 or 300 for a brute (`packs.guards`), a Warden's own adds nothing. A full clear of Altamesa is about 6000 XP, which is level 7 on the lane's curve; the Wardens alone, with every pack skipped, end around level 4. `bun run simulate --map altamesa --summary` prints the level at the end of each run (the bots only fight packs that wake on their road, so they land lower than a full clear).
