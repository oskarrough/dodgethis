# Dungeon slice

> Update 2026-10-24: the round Warden room (`maps/dungeon/`), `mechanics/boss/` and the fight report below were retired. The Warden's code now lives in `mechanics/dungeon/` (`dungeon-warden.js`, tune at `tune.warden`), and `sim.boss` became `sim.dungeon` (`encounters`, `run`). Read the sections below as history.

A WoW-style pull in the moba mode: one to five heroes fight a boss, and after every pull a fight report says why it went the way it did. Design borrowed from Balance Salvia (`docs/combat.md` there), not code. The test: does keeping that party alive still feel good after ten minutes?

Dungeon is a MOBA map on the splash, with the same online rooms as the other MOBA maps. Its party holds one to five heroes, all on the same team: go alone, invite friends, add bots, or mix them. Empty places stay empty. Its dedicated party screen suggests Salvia for you and lists all five slots. Choose your hero from your row's dropdown; the host adds or removes bots and chooses their heroes directly in each row. Each guest marks Ready, then the host starts the dungeon. Solo, Enter dungeon starts immediately. Party edits clear readiness. There are no walking controls or ready plates on this screen; the other maps keep their walk-around lobby. A map already gets heroes, casting, statuses, facts, bots, camera and HUD for free, and the contract lets a map pick its own mechanics. Names (Salvia, Warden, Mend, Ward, Slam) are placeholders until Oskar names them.

## Altamesa

Altamesa is a proof of concept for a longer dungeon: one open plateau of sand and red stone (`?mode=moba&map=altamesa&play`, in `maps/altamesa/`; the old `?map=hollowMesa` still lands here) where a party of up to five walks in from the west, fights through hand-placed packs of guards and fells a series of Wardens. Most Wardens are still placeholders with a name and a pool of health, and three bots clear the whole mesa in about two minutes.

The packs (`mechanics/packs/`) stand where the layout puts them, each made of melee, ranged and brute guards on team `N`. When any one of them notices a hero the whole pack wakes together, and if the party flees the guards follow only so far before they turn home, heal to full once they are calm, and wait. A few walk a patrol loop between two points until they spot someone. A pack that dies stays dead.

Each fight is an encounter (`encounters/<id>/`, see `extending.md`), and the layout places seven along the road: Manos, Tinaja and Botijo, Fornax, Barquero, Carillon, Espejo and Gnomon. Fornax and Barquero hold the shade wing to the north, Carillon and Espejo the sun wing to the south, and Gnomon waits at the top of the stair in the east. The dungeon run (`mechanics/dungeon/dungeon-run.js`) lets several of these pulls stand open at once. A Warden that falls stays fallen, and the heroes who died in that fight rise a few seconds later at their waystones. A wipe puts back only the Warden you were fighting. Gnomon sleeps until every Warden outside the wings is dead and one whole wing besides, and its death ends the run with a win for the party.

Manos, the hands at the gate, is the first encounter with a fight of its own (`encounters/manos/`). A wall runs across the mesa from rim to rim, and its one door stands shut until three great hands on the sand before it are lit at the same time. A hand fills while a hero stands on it and stays lit for ten seconds after they leave, so three heroes may hold one each while a lone hero must run the circuit. The first light wakes the Tiestos by the walls, who lob shards at the nearest hero; a red ring shows where each will land, and whoever still stands there is hurt. Bots share the hands out among themselves and step aside from the rings.

Tinaja and Botijo (`encounters/tinajaBotijo/`) hold Tagoror, the central court, two jars who share one potter's wheel. Pull one and both come. While they stand within a few metres of each other an ink line binds them and each takes only half of every blow, so drag them apart. When one breaks, the other goes to the wheel: it stands still and strikes no one, a red ring fills round its fallen kin, and after ten seconds the kin rises whole. Kill the second before the wheel turns. A wipe mends them both. They hit at half a Warden's weight, so one hero alone can win, and the smoke test proves it.

Fornax (`encounters/fornax/`) is the shade wing's first fight, a long hot hall where the only cool ground is the shade of an awning that walks from one end to the other and back. While Fornax is pulled, anyone in the hall outside the shade gathers Heat, a stack a second, and every stack burns; five stacks bake a hero solid for three seconds. In the shade the stacks cool away. A blue pool on the floor shows the shade, and a red ring round each hero's feet closes as their Heat builds. Fight it from the shade, and bring the fight with you as the shade moves. Bots step back into the shade at two stacks and stay until they have cooled.

Barquero (`encounters/barquero/`) ends the shade wing, and you do not fight him: the old boatman waits at his ferry for Tara, the clay idol, and you must bring it to him down a dotted road. Tara rolls only while someone stands in its ring, turning the ring blue, and coasts on for three seconds after the last one steps out. From the first push, bardinos pour over the dunes two at a time and run at whoever pushes. So one pushes while the rest keep the bardinos off, and a lone hero pushes, turns to fight, and pushes again. If the whole party falls, the bardinos scatter and Tara rolls home. Bots give the push to the first of them by name and send the rest after the bardino nearest the idol.

Carillon (`encounters/carillon/`) opens the sun wing: a Warden asleep inside a great bronze bell, which lets through only a fifth of every blow. Every ten seconds of the fight it tolls. A red ring is warned round it for two seconds, then lands, and whoever stands inside is struck hard and stunned for a second. Each toll cracks the bronze, and for six seconds after, its rim turns blue and Carillon takes half again of every blow. So stand outside the ring, then rush in and strike. Bots step out of every warned toll, the one Carillon is hitting too. (The deck has the Ball cracking the bell; that waits until the Ball can be placed by an encounter.)

Espejo (`encounters/espejo/`) closes the sun wing: a Warden with a mirror for a face, which turns slowly toward the hero it hates most. A blow struck into the mirror lands at half, and a quarter of it is thrown back at whoever struck. So one hero holds its gaze and the rest strike at its back, where the blue arc does not reach. Every twelve seconds it stops for three to polish its face, ringed in ink, and takes every blow whole; then it forgets every grudge and turns on the nearest hero. Bots that are not its target walk round to its back. Mid-pull a hurt bot fights on rather than run for a waystone, since a waystone mends no one while a fight stands open.

Each wing boss leaves a boon when it falls, and the party carries it to Gnomon: Lupa from Carillon and Tara from Barquero make Gnomon take a quarter more from every blow, while Azogue from Espejo and Bizcocho from Fornax blunt its own. The goal card in the top bar lists the boons you carry. Gnomon is still a Warden, so for now the boons are only these numbers (`encounters/gnomon/gnomon-tune.js`); its shadow comes later.

Eight waystones stand along the road. Walk up to one and it attunes, so that anyone who falls rises at the last stone you passed instead of at the gate. Stand beside it while no boss fight is on and it mends you, from nothing to full in five seconds. The hourglass in the top bar counts the run against three marks, Glass, Clay and Stone, and the goal line beside it keeps Gnomon in view. Bots fight any woken pack near them and otherwise walk to the next Warden.

## A pull

1. Your chosen party stands at the entrance of a round room. The Warden waits in the middle.
2. Someone walks within 8 m or hits it: the pull starts. Oolong grabs it and holds it, because his damage counts triple for threat.
3. The Warden swings at whoever tops its threat table. Every 16 s it winds up Slam for 2.5 s: a ring on the ground around its target. When it lands, the target takes the big hit no matter what, and anyone else still inside the ring takes splash and a bruise that ticks.
4. You Mend whoever is hurting (a 1.2 s cast that locks you in place) and Ward the target before Slam lands. Over-healing wastes your time; healing too much pulls the Warden onto you.
5. The pull ends when the Warden dies or the whole party is dead. The report opens; three seconds later the room resets (everyone back at the entrance, full health, boss full). Walk back in to pull again. The report stays open until the next pull starts. Return to party lobby on the report keeps your lineup; the pause menu also has a Party lobby action.

There is no mana (the mode cut it), so overheal costs time, not a resource: every overhealed Mend is 1.2 s you weren't healing someone else.

## Numbers (first guesses, tune after one play)

- Salvia (`heroes/salvia/`): 1400 hp, speed 5, basic `tennisBall` (reused). Slot1 Mend, slot2 Ward. No slot3.
- Mend (`abilities/mend/`): `kind: 'instant'`, picks the living ally nearest the aim point within 10 m (copy Tag out's `targetUnit`), `castPoint: 1.2`, `cooldown: 0`, heals 300, `critChance: 0.1`, `critMultiplier: 1.5`. Max 250 hps.
- Ward (`abilities/ward/`): same targeting, `castPoint: 0`, `cooldown: 12`, applies `ward.shield` (`absorb: true`, `bar: 'shield'`, copy `swig.shield`) of 500 for 8 s.
- Warden (`mechanics/boss/`, tune in `maps/dungeon/dungeon-tune.js` under `boss`): 15000 hp, radius 1.2, walks straight at its target at 3.5 m/s (the room has no obstacles, so no pathing), aggro 8 m.
- Swing (`abilities/swing/`): 150 to the threat target every 1.5 s, reach 2.5 m, 0.4 s tell. About 100 dps on the tank.
- Slam (`abilities/slam/`): first 10 s into the pull, then every 16 s. Windup 2.5 s: the Warden stops moving and swinging, and the ring's centre locks on the target's position at windup start. On land: 700 to the target (wherever it stands), 350 plus `slam.bruise` (periodic, 50 per second for 6 s, `tags: ['debuff']`, refresh) to every other party hero inside 4 m of the centre.
- Threat (`dungeon-tune.js` `threat`): damage to the boss adds `damage × multiplier[heroId] ?? 1` (`multiplier: { oolong: 3 }`); effective healing (overheal excluded) adds `heal: 0.5 ×` to the boss while engaged; absorbs add nothing. The boss switches only when a challenger beats its current target by `switch: 1.1`.
- Sanity: Oolong (1800 hp) takes about 160 dps averaged with Slam; Mend covers 250 hps if you cast nonstop, so Ward plus a good pre-Slam top-up is what keeps him up. Party damage around 330 dps makes a pull about 45 s, three Slams.

## Effects: statuses are the one system

`statuses.js` already is Balance Salvia's aura system in small: refresh, replace and capped stacks per source, tick-based `periodic` hooks, `absorb` drained oldest first, live `damageTaken` modifiers, tags and cleanse. Ward's shield, Slam's bruise and any later buff are status declarations in their ability folder; nothing new to build, only to log. Two gaps get filled: every apply, refresh and removal becomes a fact, and an absorb shield that falls off unspent reports what it left (`wasted`, Balance Salvia's overheal for shields).

Crits: Balance Salvia has none yet (its docs list crit as a later slice; sweet-spot heals only look like crits). Dodgethis already has deterministic basic crits (`critEvery`) and `hit.crit`. Heals get a roll on their own seeded stream (`critChance`; isolated from the sim's `rng` so no existing draw moves and replays agree) inside the heal door, logged on the `heal` fact.

## Facts

Facts already carry `tick`. Ids only: the `pull` start fact carries the roster with names, so the report needs no game constants and no lookups.

| fact                  | fields                                                                                                                                                                | emitted in                                           |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| `pull` (new)          | `state: 'start'`, `step` (seconds per tick), `party: [{ id, heroId, name, maxHp }]`, `boss: { id, name, maxHp }`, `abilities: { id: name }`                           | `mechanics/boss/boss-pull.js` on engage              |
| `pull` (new)          | `state: 'end'`, `result: 'killed' \| 'wiped'`, `bossHp`, `threat: { id: value }`                                                                                      | same, on boss death or wipe                          |
| `heal` (new)          | `source`, `target`, `ability`, `amount` (landed), `overheal`, `crit`, `periodic`, `hp`, `maxHp`                                                                       | `combat.js` `heal()`, the one door                   |
| `hit` (extend)        | add `absorbed` (eaten by shields), `periodic`, `status` (key when a tick caused it); existing `damage`, `crit`, `hp`, `maxHp`, `ability`                              | `combat.js` `hit`                                    |
| `absorb` (new)        | `source` (shield caster), `target`, `key`, `status` (instance id), `amount`                                                                                           | `statuses.js` `absorb`                               |
| `status` (new)        | `state: 'on' \| 'refresh' \| 'off'`, `target`, `source`, `key`, `label`, `status` (instance id), `amount`, `until`; on `off` also `reason` and `wasted` (absorb left) | `statuses.js` `apply`, `remove`                      |
| `cast` (extend)       | add `lock` (seconds committed: the cast point) and `targetId`                                                                                                         | `casting.js` cast start; boss swing and slam windups |
| `castCancelled` (new) | `hero`, `ability`, `slot`                                                                                                                                             | `control.js` stop/cancel during a cast point         |
| `threat` (new)        | `unit` (boss), `target` (new top), `previous`, `values: { id: value }` rounded                                                                                        | `boss-pull.js`, only when the boss changes target    |

`death` stays as is. Every new type joins `FACTS` in `index.js`.

## The fight report

Built by a pure reducer, `reduceFight(facts) → report` in `mechanics/boss/boss-report.js` (facts in, numbers out, no imports from the sim), drawn by `boss-view.js` as an `aside` reusing the death recap's classes in `hud.css`. Seconds are `tick × step` from the start fact. A shield still live at pull start counts as one cast; remaining at start is shield given.

1. Header: Killed or Wiped, duration, Warden health left.
2. Party table, one row per hero: damage done, healing done, overheal %, shield absorbed, shield wasted %, damage taken, deaths, hurt (share of the pull below 30% health), busy (sum of cast locks, a cancelled cast counting up to its cancel, over the duration).
3. Salvia's spells: casts, total, average, crits, overheal % per ability.
4. Each death: the last five hits and heals on that hero before it died, with time, ability and source name.
5. Threat: who held the Warden for how long, and every switch ("Warden turned to Salvia at 34.2 s").
6. Wasted casts: each Mend that overhealed by 80% or more, with its time ("Mend at 14.9 s (93%)").

Later, not now: a health timeline you can scrub, saved pulls to compare.

**S1.
