# dodgethis redesign proposal

## What is here now

From the code, dodgethis is a bright toy court suspended in a blue void: an 11 × 24 green slab, a cream center stripe, blue/red capsules with white facing nubs, thin modeled arrows, and a purple rolling bowl (`court.js`, `player.js`, `arrow.js`). `render.js` gives these ordinary lit materials, cast shadows, fog, and a fixed elevated perspective camera, not an orthographic one. The hub's scalloped, navy-outlined portal stickers and the chunky HTML buttons already suggest a more authored comic world than the largely undecorated match. Movement has acceleration, hard stopping, and a committed 0.12-second dash; the human charges a ping-pong bow meter while bots fetch, kite, and shoot. Contrary to the older plan's aim description, the pointer now selects direction and charge selects range; the dotted arc predicts touchdown, while the bowl has only a range guide. Seven shared ammo objects, automatic pickup, one-hit elimination, and best-of-three matches preserve the grab-versus-shoot tension. Shots have trails, synth cues, and camera jitter; pickups have a sample cue; deaths randomly melt, topple, or dissolve with particles before the result card. Those are code-derived observations, not a playtest or listening assessment: the promising feel is quick movement punctuated by theatrical exits, but grounded ammo and armed silhouettes receive much less emphasis than menus and deaths.

## Geometry-first stylized rendering

The confirmed direction is a comic-sticker/printed playground: flat team colors, navy ink edges, cream highlights, and restrained halftone shading. Extend the portal's existing language. The geometry-first surface-data/style pass will become the game's sole renderer. Keep the open court, camera framing, projectile physics, and charge rules unchanged during the visual work.

- Start with the information that matters most: give grounded arrows a small cream/navy diamond backing, distinct from the circular landing reticle. It disappears immediately on pickup or launch. This is visual geometry only, not a larger pickup radius or collider. Keep the marker still; it need not pulse.
- Give the player a readable bow-shaped attachment and a stronger facing silhouette. Distinguish armed and empty poses, and add a non-color team badge so ally and enemy identity survives grayscale. Keep the capsule collision envelope honest; decorative limbs must not suggest a much larger target.
- Introduce a small `src/style.js` with semantic roles for court, ink, cream, teams, ammo, bowl, and perfect release. Feed both Three materials and CSS custom properties from that palette at boot, with DOM-free defaults for tests. Replace scattered colors in `round.js`, `player.js`, `arrow.js`, `weapons.js`, `weaponhud.js`, `portal.js`, `death.js`, and `godmodefx.js`; preserve the meaning shared by preview and trail. Perfect remains a release-quality accent, not an invented damage class.
- Replace the direct rendering path in `render.js` and `main.js` with a surface-data pipeline and a focused new `src/stylepass.js`. Opaque surfaces write shade, discrete style ID, and an explicitly encoded two-channel normal into a render target with depth. Keep data linear and IDs nearest-sampled; do not tone-map or blend encoded IDs. The final shader applies two or three shade bands, depth/normal edges, one world-anchored halftone rule, and subtle stable irregularity. Manage target resize, pixel ratio, disposal, and final color conversion explicitly.
- Do not send every object through that opaque buffer unchanged. Trails, the dashed preview, portal labels/sparkles, fading corpses, and the godmode bubble need a depth-aware forward composition path. Reserve crisp treatment for aiming and pickups; do not let outlines or dots swallow a distant arrow. Death tint/opacity currently mutate material fields directly, so `death.js` needs compatible presentation parameters rather than assuming replacement shaders behave like `MeshStandardMaterial`.
- Keep DOM text outside WebGL. `index.html`, `weaponhud.js`, and `overlay.js` should share the palette and hard ink edges without shader-distorting text or weakening focus states. Keep the existing combat log and debug GUI available as inspection tools; world feedback must communicate outcomes without relying on them.

I would not adopt a library of interchangeable media, downloaded asset packs, full deferred lighting, bloom, animated film grain, or hatching over every surface. One medium is enough; noise that competes with scarce ammo defeats the game. Nor would I throw away the useful canvas-texture portal labels to satisfy a literal “no textures” rule. Readability and the agreed device budget are requirements of the new renderer itself.

## Layered game feel and feedback

Use successful gameplay actions as the synchronization points. Today `round.js` directly calls audio, combat logging, and shake, while `player.js` starts death presentation and `arrow.js` silently settles shots. A small injected callback is enough to connect these; no global event-bus framework is needed.

- Add a `present(event)` service, wired by `main.js` to a new `src/feedback.js`. Emit shot, pickup, impact, fall, accepted dash, and round-result facts only after their gameplay transition succeeds. Carry relevant IDs, source team/local ownership, weapon kind, perfect release, position, direction, surface, and outcome; omit meaningless fields rather than inventing health or damage amounts.
- Capture impact data before `unit.eliminate()` removes the collider and `arrow.ground()` destroys the projectile body and resets its kind. Copy body position and velocity rather than retaining mutable vectors. Rapier's current collision-start callback supplies handles, not a contact point: obtain contact geometry before removal where available, otherwise explicitly use a projectile-position approximation. Do not place a decal at an assumed surface contact. Distinguish actual touchdown from an off-court landing clamped back onto the platform; the latter is ammo recovery, not an invisible wall impact.
- On a confirmed elimination, immediately snap the target into a recognizable out pose/tint, add a short directional ink burst, play contact audio, and show a brief local hit confirmation. Then let the existing death flourish recover and disappear. All ordinary hits are lethal here; a perfect shot may miss, and a godmode deflection must not produce an elimination marker. Falls have their own downward response, without a fictitious arrow contact.
- Give the pickup a small inward snap and immediate ARMED emphasis; give the accepted dash a short directional streak and recovery lean. Add modest visual recoil and charge anticipation to the bow. Use damped springs only on visual children, never on collider motion or input. First separate the visual child from the logical root in `player.js`: gameplay currently reads `mesh.position` and `handPosition()`, so cosmetic movement must not perturb launch points, AI targeting, or the preview.
- Replace per-death particle allocations in `death.js` with bounded reusable slots in `feedback.js`, sharing the world's palette and mark shapes. Start with a concrete budget, such as 64 particles and 16 short-lived floor marks; recycle the oldest decorative slot and clear all round-owned effects on restart. Surface-aligned floor marks must stay quieter than pickups. Keep an authored collapse curve rather than making every death springy.
- Retain the existing synth/sample mix in `audio.js` and `src/sfx/`. Add position-based stereo and distance gain for world cues, cap overlapping bot chatter, and reserve the strongest confirmation for local outcomes. A preloaded contact cue must not wait for a sample decode. If synthesized noise is introduced, cache its buffer. `input.js` can expose active-device information; `weaponhud.js` and `overlay.js` should show appropriate prompts rather than always advertising mouse/keyboard controls.
- Always run the normal game animations and effects, regardless of OS motion preferences. Do not add a reduced-motion path. Any camera impulse must leave pointer aiming stable, using an unshaken aiming camera or equivalent projection separation in `main.js` and `render.js`.

I would not adopt health flashes, critical-damage tiers, first-person sway/FOV kicks, physical knockback, or constant shake: there is no health system, this is an overview camera, and displacement changes dodge fairness. Defer hit-stop until contact without it is convincing. The current charge/death presentation runs on frame time while Rapier and round-end delay use scaled fixed time; freezing only physics would change charge opportunities and verdict timing. If later justified for a deciding hit, design those clocks together, keep menus/input responsive, and prevent paused time from becoming catch-up simulation. No networking layer is needed for this local game.

## Procedural game construction kits

Deepen the few builders already present rather than adding a general construction engine. `buildCourt()` already creates matching visible and collision geometry, `createPlayer()` uses the same dimensions for capsule and collider, and bowl radius controls both representations. The missing shared truth is mostly arena policy and presentation identity, not a shortage of primitive wrappers.

- Make a DOM-free arena description in a new `src/arena.js`: court dimensions/top height, kill plane, spawn regions, pickup scatter region, and explicitly named landing/dash/AI safety insets. Derive bounds helpers from this description. Different insets are intentional gameplay policies, not numbers to collapse into one universal margin.
- `court.js` builds the slab, stripe, and readable rim from it; `round.js` derives roster and ammo placement; `arrow.js` and the preview use its landing clamp; `player.js` uses its dash bounds; `ai.js` derives rim steering and strafe limits. Changing width or depth once should update all of these, including the hard-coded ±8 spawn locations. Preserve normal walking off the edge and dash clamping unless Oskar explicitly changes those rules.
- Let `portal.js` take a small description that owns label, palette role, visible activation boundary, trigger radius, and wake radius. Keep the scalloped decorative surround distinct from the activation zone. This is a visual pad with a proximity trigger, not a solid object that should secretly create a collider.
- Keep `createPlayer`, `createArrow`, and `createPortal` as the game vocabulary. Separate static court construction from dynamic unit/weapon visuals. Share or merge repeated static geometry by style where measurement justifies it; do not merge moving labels or characters into an arena mesh. Make ownership clear: persistent renderer/court resources survive rounds; rounds release their bodies, dynamic visuals, and effects; instances never dispose shared palette materials.
- Add a seeded setup option for reproducible ammo placement and visual review fixtures. Keep decoration randomness separate from gameplay randomness so changing sparkles cannot change a match. This is not a promise of deterministic replays; bot randomness would also need control for those.

I would not adopt city/building builders, an ECS, a navmesh, or a spatial index for a handful of units and seven arrows. `spatial.js` already provides the appropriate linear nearest scan. No center net, cover, or new arrow types belong in this presentation redesign: obstacles would invalidate straight-line AI and introduce a separate rules/navigation project. If that project is approved later, its obstacle description must own collision and AI constraints together.

## Sequence

Each step is a separate playable change with a focused review, not a prerequisite branch that stays invisible until the renderer is finished. Preserve the existing motion, ballistic/charge, and headless round checks throughout; use `bun run check` and `bun run build` during implementation, not as a substitute for browser review. No implementation or package changes are part of this proposal.

1. Make scarce ammo unmistakable. In `arrow.js`, add the grounded-only diamond backing and ensure every held/flying/grounded transition and disposal handles it. Review all seven pickups from near and far ends of the unchanged court, with HUD hidden and sound muted, plus hit drops and bowl settlement. Ship without any shader or physics change.

2. Make a hit readable at contact. Add the minimal presentation callback in `round.js`/`main.js`, snapshot impact facts before teardown, and coordinate immediate out pose, ink burst, sound, and confirmation through `feedback.js`/`death.js`. Use the bounded effect pool from the outset. Review a miss/touchdown, ordinary lethal hit, perfect miss/hit, godmode deflection, fall, and simultaneous wipe; events must not duplicate or change winner/ammo results.

3. Connect preparation and recovery. In `player.js`, isolate cosmetic transforms, then add accepted-dash streak/lean, shot recoil, and pickup emphasis through the same feedback path. Update `audio.js`, `input.js`, `weaponhud.js`, and `overlay.js` for restrained spatial cues and device-aware prompts. Controller rumble is out of scope. Review mute, disconnected gamepad, stalled-tab recovery, and unchanged launch/aim positions; OS motion preferences must not suppress effects. No hit-stop yet.

4. Establish shared arena truth with a visible rim. Add `arena.js` and update court, spawns, landing clamps, dash limits, and AI boundaries together; add a thin visual rim warning with no collision. Review the normal arena and a narrower test fixture: mesh/collider agree, spawns fit, ammo remains reachable, bots turn away from edges, and walking off still falls. Restore normal dimensions for shipping; include seeded setup for repeatable later comparisons.

5. Unify silhouettes and color without post-processing. Add `style.js`, the readable bow/armed pose and non-color team badges; reconcile previews, particles, portals, godmode, and HTML accents. Review flat silhouettes and grayscale captures at the far court end. Keep collision dimensions, movement, and projectile behavior untouched. This establishes the semantic materials and shape language that the style pass will consume.

6. Replace the renderer outright. Build the surface-data/style pass in `render.js`/`stylepass.js` and route `main.js` through it for every match and hub frame, removing the old direct rendering path. Develop against a small fixture using the real court, one moving player, an arrow, and an impact, then complete depth-aware forward composition for labels, trails, deaths, and shield in the same change. Review depth edges, stable halftones during camera motion, thin projectiles, resize/high-DPI behavior, and frame cost on the agreed low-end device. Review one- and three-foe matches, perfect shots and bowls, repeated hub/restart cycles, effect-budget saturation, and stable GPU resources. Ship one coherent renderer, with readability and performance issues resolved within that pipeline.

## Questions for Oskar

- What is the lowest-end browser/device and target frame rate the new renderer must support?
- Is the bowl a permanent equal partner to the bow, or an experiment whose HUD and silhouette should remain secondary? This proposal preserves it either way.
- Should enemies visibly telegraph their existing aim interval, making dodge timing easier to read, or should the redesign avoid revealing information the current capsule hides?
- Can we standardize the immediate elimination pose and make extreme spinning death variants optional, while keeping a little comic variety after the outcome is clear?
